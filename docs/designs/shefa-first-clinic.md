# Design: SHEFA First-Clinic Strategy — Shadow Line + Door-Opener

## Problem Statement

Bangladeshi clinics run a single front-desk employee who juggles walk-in
customers, appointment books for multiple doctors, cash handling, and acting
as the doctor's personal assistant. During rush hours — or any time the doctor
summons them — the phone rings unanswered. Genuine patients trying to book an
appointment retry 4-5 times; some give up and go to a competing clinic. The
user estimates a "renowned clinic" receives 2,000+ appointment calls
collectively across departments. No formal call-volume statistics exist.

The user is building this from OUTSIDE the market: a self-built, fully-local
AI medical receptionist voice agent (SHEFA) that already supports Bangla and
English, runs fully on-premises (Python + local LLM qwen2.5:7b + local Whisper
STT + local Bangla VITS and Piper TTS + Twilio telephony), and has no named
clinic owner, no users, and no measured demand evidence yet.

## Demand Evidence

Honest baseline: NO direct evidence yet. Nobody at any clinic has asked for
this; no live experiment has run. This session classified it as a conviction
sale: the pitch will live or die on whether the pain can be proven inside a
real org, not on anyone saying the idea sounds good.

## Status Quo

Known in detail from lived market knowledge (not from inside a clinic):

- One overloaded desk employee handles walk-ins, multi-doctor appointment
  books, and cash simultaneously.
- When the doctor calls the employee away mid-task, incoming calls ring out.
- Patients retry 4-5 times during rush hours; some leave for other clinics.
- 2,000+ appointment calls across all departments at a renowned clinic
  (estimate; unmeasured).

These numbers are the anchor of the ROI story but are UNMEASURED. The retry
pattern likely overcounts unique lost bookings.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

No specific org is named yet — the binding constraint this session was agreed
to be "find the first clinic first," not the product or the feature.

Target user chain: the front-desk employee (demo subject), the clinic owner
(buyer/decision-maker), the patient (who defects when calls drop).

Narrowest wedge: let the owner say yes to "catch calls the desk misses" —
week one proves the capture; confirmed bookings are demonstrated via the demo
and piloted only after the owner's yes — parked next to the existing HMS.

## Constraints

- Pre-product: no users, no named clinic, no demand evidence.
- Conviction sale: evidence must be manufactured, not referenced.
- Product is a secondary/complementary software to an existing incumbent
  hospital management system (HMS) — deliberately not replacing it day one.
- Bangla-first clinic operation; SHEFA must prove the Bangla voice loop.
- Hardware is fully local (RTX 4060 Ti 16GB) — appropriate for data-stays-on-premises positioning, but limits concurrency for large call volumes.
- Building from outside the market; no org in reach yet.
- Pilot concurrency target: 1-2 simultaneous calls on the local GPU; adequate
  for the pilot but NOT for the 2,000+-calls/month volume claim, which is
  deferred until after the first yes.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: The Door-Opener (minimal viable)
48-hour build on the existing stack: fixed booking script, LLM as slot-filler,
Bangla VITS, one Twilio line, SQLite call log + minimal dashboard view,
60-second recorded demo + one-page Bangla pitch to 10-20 named clinics.
Effort S, Low risk. Fastest path to a real ringing line and demo theater, but
undersells SHEFA's dialogue and carries no data on its own.

### Approach B: The Booking Engine (ideal architecture / land-and-expand)
SHEFA becomes source of truth for confirmed appointments with per-doctor
availability, feeding the incumbent HMS via one-line paste-in. Effort L, Medium
risk. Builds the moat (trusted booking record) and maps to the AI-first HMS
roadmap, but is the most speculative pre-door; HMS constraints are unverifiable
until inside a clinic.

### Approach C: The Shadow Line (creative/lateral)
Do not pitch SHEFA yet. Install a passive overflow capture line that logs every
call the desk misses for one week, then walk in with the clinic's OWN real
numbers (missed calls, estimated lost bookings). Demo agent is the second act
pitched with their data on the table. Effort M, Low risk. Manufactures the
missing demand evidence and satisfies premise #2's measurement precondition as
a side effect. Week one earns log data, not bookings; requires owner permission
for a passive line.

C's "missed" trigger: the clinic forwards its published line to the shadow
number on no-answer after N rings. N is set LOW (2 rings) so callers who bail
early stay in the log. KNOWN BLIND SPOT: callers who hang up before the
2-ring trigger never enter the log, so the abandonment distribution is
truncated at the bottom — bracket this when comparing the log against the
2,000+ claim. Log schema per call: caller ID, timestamp, duration,
transcript, disposition flag (booking intent / general / wrong number), and
whether it was resolved mid-week by the desk.

## Recommended Approach

**A + C in parallel this week.**

Rationale: The binding constraint is the first door, and evidence-manufacturing
(Shadow Line) plus demo-theater (Door-Opener) are the two independent wedges
that open it. C converts premise #2's measurement precondition into the
doorstep story; A supplies the emotional close ("hear a machine complete a
Bangla booking on your own line"). Running them in parallel beats sequencing
because each de-risks the other: the log data makes the demo credible, the demo
makes the data interesting.

Sequencing note: if parallel execution is too heavy for one week, the fallback
order is C first (evidence), then A (theater) — but the chosen posture is both.

## Data Handling & Privacy (health telecom data)

The shadow line records and transcribes REAL PATIENT CALLS. It is health
telecom data and must be governed before week one:

- Caller disclosure: the answered line must state clearly (Bangla) that "this
  line is being recorded to improve clinic service." No silent transcription.
- Retention: transcripts held only for the measurement week, then deleted
  T+14 days after the measurement week unless the owner opts into keeping
  them.
- Access during the pilot: the owner sees "today's calls" via a read-only view
  over a private tunnel or a periodic pasted/printed summary — never raw
  transcripts sent over public channels. This access path is stated in the
  one-pager. Shown to owner + builder only; nothing else leaves the box.
- Hardware location is PINNED for week one: the shadow line runs on the
  BUILDER's local machine (no clinic box exists yet), so patient call audio
  and transcripts physically live on the builder's hardware during the
  measurement week. The one-pager consent must state this explicitly — data
  staying on-site is the END STATE, not the week-one reality. The box moves to
  clinic premises only after the first yes.
- The outreach ask must SHOW this policy in the one-pager — consent is part of
  the yes, not a fine print after it.
- This section is not a substitute for country-specific telecom consent law;
  verify the Bangladesh requirement before the first forwarded call.

## Success Criteria

- One named clinic owner gives live-line access to a shadow capture line
  within two weeks of outreach.
- One week of real missed-call log data collected from that line, testing
  premise #2's volume claims against reality.
- If measured missed-call volume comes in far below the 2,000+/4-5-retries
  story, the ROI narrative pivots to staff-time/abandoned-call math (per the
  measurement precondition) — do NOT stretch the original claims.
- A working Bangla booking loop (call -> greeting -> booking slots -> caller
  info -> confirmation) demonstrated live on the local stack.
- One clinic owner hears a completed Bangla booking demo and says yes to a
  pilot of the answering agent.

## Go / No-Go Gate

This is a conviction sale; it needs an explicit decision point, not endless
pitch. After 2 weeks and outreach to 10-20 clinics:
- GO: >=1 owner granted shadow-line access OR committed to a pilot demo.
- NARROW: 0 qualified yeses but warm referrals exist in the outreach list —
  re-run outreach through those referrals for another week before any next
  move.
- STOP/CLOSE: 0 yeses, 0 warm referrals — the thesis is not landing on its own;
  suspend the wedge, or reframe the pitch object (e.g., patient-experience
  story instead of missed-call ROI) and only then retry fresh.
