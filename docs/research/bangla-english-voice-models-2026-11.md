# Keep the measured stack, then fine-tune

SHEFA should ship on a 12 GB card with the models the project has already measured, plus one cheap addition for English. The dialogue model stays **gemma2:9b**, the only model with 12/12 tool accuracy in the project's own bench, and **Gemma 4 E4B** (Apache 2.0) is benchmarked as its likely successor. Speech-to-text stays the **OmiKhan Bangla Whisper-medium** fine-tune (Apache-2.0, 16.5% WER, 1.6 GB), and a small stock Whisper is added for {bn, en} language ID and English turns. Text-to-speech stays **Piper bn_BD-google-medium** on CPU for Bangla, and **Kokoro-82M** (Apache-2.0) is added for English. Together these sit near **10 GB**, leaving about 2 GB of headroom on a headless 12 GB card. The weakest link for a client who cares most about natural conversation is the voice: nobody on the team has listened to the Piper output yet. No open Bangla voice combines a Bangladeshi accent, a clean commercial licence and high naturalness. The highest-value fine-tuning is therefore, in order: a QLoRA of the LLM on reviewed synthetic receptionist dialogues, a telephone-augmented Whisper LoRA on Bangladeshi data, and a Piper voice trained on 1-3 hours of a consenting Bangladeshi voice talent. All three fit on 12 GB. For client demos, **Azure's free tier** gives real bn-BD neural voices and the **Groq and Gemini free tiers** cover the LLM. Groq's free Whisper is stock Whisper, though, and stock large-v3 scored 70% WER on Bangla in the project's bench, so it can only serve English turns. One caution applies to every number here: the project's bench ran on an RTX 4060 Ti **16 GB**, not the 12 GB target card, so every VRAM figure must be re-measured on the real hardware.

## The measured stack is still the best local choice

The strongest evidence available is the project's own bench, which tested models on the router prompt and on real and phone-simulated Bengali audio. No public benchmark ranks current small LLMs on Bangla *generation*. Public ASR benchmarks exclude telephone audio. Public TTS MOS figures are self-reported and not comparable across models. Measured project numbers therefore outrank leaderboard claims, and a new model replaces an incumbent only after it passes the same harness.

| Role | Use now | Bench next (in order) | Licence of the pick | VRAM of the pick | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dialogue LLM | gemma2:9b, JSON mode, thinking n/a | gemma4:e4b (think off); qwen3.5:9b (think off); gemma3:12b-it-qat text-only | Gemma Terms (not OSI) | 7.2 GB measured | Only model with 12/12 right tool, 1.0 s median reply |
| Bangla STT | OmiKhan/bangla-asr-whisper-medium, CTranslate2 int8 | hishab titu Conformer-large (licence first); Omnilingual CTC_300M/1B v2 | Apache-2.0 | 1.6 GB measured | 4x better than stock large-v3 on Bengali |
| English STT + LID | stock Whisper small (or small.en) int8 | large-v3-turbo int8; distil-large-v3 | MIT | 0.6 GB measured (small) | Bangla fine-tunes rarely output Latin-script English |
| Bangla TTS | Piper bn_BD-google-medium, CPU | Indic Parler-TTS; Svara-TTS v1; custom Piper voice | CC-BY-SA 4.0 per project doc | 0 GB (CPU, RTF 0.30) | Only licence-clean Bangla voice that loads today |

### Dialogue LLM: keep gemma2:9b, test Gemma 4 E4B against it

In the project bench, **gemma2:9b scored 12/12 valid JSON, 12/12 right tool and 11/12 right arguments, with 95% Bangla purity, a 1.0 s median reply and 7.2 GB VRAM**. Gemma 3 12B produced the cleanest Bangla (99.6% purity) but missed one tool and used 10.3 GB, which does not fit beside ASR on 12 GB as configured. Qwen3-8B collapsed to **7% purity** because its thinking mode leaked English into replies ([model-selection.md](../model-selection.md)). Two releases since that bench change the shortlist. **Gemma 4** arrived in April 2026 under **Apache 2.0**, the first Gemma under an OSI licence ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/4/google-releases-gemma-4-open-weight-ai-models-with-apache-2-0-license)). Its E4B variant is about **5.3 GB at Q4_K_M** ([HF GGUF](https://huggingface.co/AlexAtomic/gemma4-e4b-it-GGUF)), but it scores only **42.2% on Tau2** against 68.2% for the 26B MoE, according to a mirrored model card ([HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/alekshandru/gemma-4-E4B)). Weak agentic scores are a warning about tool routing, which is exactly where gemma2:9b leads. **Qwen3.5-9B** (Apache 2.0, March 2026) has thinking **off by default** on the small sizes ([Emelia](https://emelia.io/hub/qwen-35-9b-review)), which removes the Qwen3 failure mode. Hosts report BFCL-V4 66.1 and TAU2 79.1 for it ([DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)), but Qwen models have historically trailed Gemma on natural Bangla.

The Bangla-specific models do not fit the role. TigerLLM-9B scored 75% purity and 9/12 tools in the project bench, and its HF card says the published 9B weights "are not the final checkpoints" ([HF TigerLLM-9B-it](https://huggingface.co/dipta007/TigerLLM-9B-it)). Aya Expanse scored 6/12 tools and 80% purity. TituLLM is only 1-3B. The rule for switching is simple: adopt a challenger only if it reaches at least 11/12 tools, at least 98% purity and under 1 s to first token on the expanded bilingual bench. If it does, Gemma 4 E4B also frees about 1 GB and removes the Gemma Terms caveat.

### Speech-to-text: the Bangla model is fine, English is the gap

The OmiKhan fine-tune measured **16.5% WER on clean FLEURS, 17.7% on phone-band audio and 22.7% with phone plus noise**, against **70.0%** for stock Whisper large-v3 ([model-selection.md](../model-selection.md)). The most rigorous public comparison, a 12-model benchmark from October 2026, puts public Whisper-medium Bangla fine-tunes at 13.9% FLEURS WER. The best public model there, **hishab/titu_stt_bn_conformer_large**, reaches **10.83% WER at 87 ms per clip against 304 ms for Whisper-medium**. It collapses on conversational speech, though (29.30% on IndicVoices, 33.10% on Vaani), and its licence is unconfirmed while its sibling FastConformer is CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([ehzawad benchmark](https://github.com/ehzawad/bengali-asr-benchmark); [HF hishab](https://huggingface.co/hishab/titu_stt_bn_fastconformer/discussions)). The same benchmark found that skipping Unicode NFC normalisation inflates Whisper-medium's FLEURS WER from **16.2% to 27.9%**. The project's 16.5% must be confirmed as NFC-normalised before any comparison. Switching base model buys a few points at most. Conversational and telephone fine-tuning is the bigger lever. Meta's **Omnilingual ASR** (Apache-2.0) is the cleanest-licensed challenger: CTC_300M needs about 2 GiB and CTC_1B about 3 GiB. Its only Bengali figure, 2.8% CER, is for the 7B model, and inputs are capped at 40 s ([omnilingual-asr](https://github.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr)).

English and code-switching are where the current setup is weakest. In the SPRING-INX set, about half the references contain Latin-script English words, and **no model in the 12-model benchmark reproduced them** ([ehzawad benchmark](https://github.com/ehzawad/bengali-asr-benchmark)). Fine-tuning Whisper on one low-resource language makes it forget English after little training ([ComputEL 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/2025.computel-main.18.pdf)). So an English-speaking caller needs a separate path. Run stock Whisper for language ID, restricted to the argmax over only {bn, en} so a Bengali caller cannot be labelled Hindi or Assamese. Whisper-small's default LID got Bengali right about **87%** of the time (366 of 418) with clip length uncontrolled ([arXiv 2403.17314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17314)). Send English turns to an English-capable model. The same stock small model (0.6 GB measured) is the cheapest English path. large-v3-turbo int8 is the stronger option at an estimated 1.5-2 GB, scaled from faster-whisper's measured **2,926 MB for large-v2 int8** ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). Banglish turns, where Bangla carries English medical words, stay on the Bangla model. Those words will come out in Bangla script (অ্যাপয়েন্টমেন্ট), so the LLM prompt must accept them in that form.

### Text-to-speech: a working voice nobody has listened to yet

Piper bn_BD-google-medium is the current voice. It has a **round-trip WER of 14.6%** (human speech scores 16-17% with the same judge) and a **real-time factor of 0.30 on CPU**. Its licence is recorded as CC-BY-SA 4.0. The project doc states plainly that "nobody on the team has yet listened to the TTS output" ([model-selection.md](../model-selection.md)). The research could not find this voice in Piper's official voice list. One third-party mirror labels it CC-BY-4.0 in a misaligned table ([Piper VOICES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/main/docs/VOICES.md); [PYTHAI mirror](https://huggingface.co/PYTHAI/voaice)), so assume the stricter share-alike terms until the MODEL_CARD is read.

The alternatives each fail on one axis. The MIT-tagged **bangla-speech-processing VITS** voices self-report MOS 4.1 ([README](https://huggingface.co/bangla-speech-processing/bangla_tts_female/blob/main/README.md)), and a VITS-Bengali voice reached **MOS 4.49** in the BanglaTalk study ([arXiv 2510.06188](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06188v1)). The project found them unusable as published, though: their config files are 0 bytes and the repo has no licence file. **Meta MMS-TTS-ben** is CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([Mixpeek](https://mixpeek.com/model/facebook/mms-tts-ben)). **Indic Parler-TTS** (Apache-2.0 reported, 21 Indic languages) is labelled "slow" by aggregators ([tts.ai](https://tts.ai/voices/indic-parler/?lang=as)). **Svara-TTS v1** (Apache-2.0, Bengali plus Indian English) is a 3B Orpheus model at about **1.9 GB in 4-bit** before codec and KV cache ([mlx-audio Svara](https://blaizzy.github.io/mlx-audio/models/tts/svara/)). Both are trained on Indian-curated corpora, so they will probably sound West Bengal-accented. Kokoro, the stock Chatterbox-Multilingual and XTTS-family models do not support Bengali at all ([hexgrad/kokoro](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro)).

For English, **Kokoro-82M** is the clear pick. It has 82M parameters, Apache-2.0 weights, 24 kHz output and a community-measured CPU RTF of about 0.205 ([hexgrad/kokoro](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro); [community benchmark](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sqkrqp/snapshots/2026-04-21T06%3A28%3A59.170586Z)). The cost of a two-voice design is that the Bangla and English voices are different speakers. Pick a Kokoro voice close in gender and pitch to the Piper voice, and keep English words inside Bangla turns on the Bangla voice by writing them in Bangla script. The first action costs nothing: run a blind listening test with Bangladeshi staff on 20 receptionist prompts. Compare Piper, Indic Parler, Svara and Azure bn-BD-NabanitaNeural as a reference, and score accent, numerals and English loanwords.

Configuration A is the safe default. It uses only measured parts and leaves room for KV growth and allocator fragmentation. Configuration B swaps the 7.2 GB LLM for Gemma 4 E4B and spends the saving on a much better English recogniser. That is the right upgrade if E4B holds tool accuracy. Configuration C reaches the cleanest measured Bangla, but only by stripping Gemma 3 12B down to QAT int4 weights (6.6 GB, against an 8.9 GB Ollama tag) with no vision projector, a 4k context and a q8_0 KV cache ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/19/gemma-3-qat-models/)). That leaves almost nothing for spikes. Configuration D shows why expressive GPU TTS is off the table locally: any GPU voice of Svara or Indic Parler size pushes the stack past 12 GB unless the LLM moves to the cloud. The server settings that make these numbers hold are `num_ctx 4096`, `OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION=1`, `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0`, `keep_alive -1`, one resident LLM, ASR loaded first, and no display on the inference GPU.

## Edge cases are handled in code around the models

The client's second priority, edge-case handling, depends less on model choice than on the guard rails around each model. The project bench already caught one such failure: `parse_action` rejected valid JSON wrapped in a Markdown fence and **silently dropped bookings and emergency transfers** ([model-selection.md](../model-selection.md)). On the LLM side, grammar-constrained decoding makes JSON validity a non-issue: every benched model reached 11/12 or 12/12 valid JSON. Put the spoken reply inside the action object (`{"tool", "args", "say"}`) so one call yields both, and keep thinking explicitly off for Gemma 4 and Qwen3.5. Numbers are the most common audible failure in Bangla TTS. The LLM should emit ISO dates, times and phone numbers in `args`, and code should render them deterministically as Bangla words ("বিকেল সাড়ে চারটা", phone numbers digit by digit). The LLM should never be trusted to spell numbers. BanglaKontho ships a Bangla text normaliser that could seed this, though its data is non-commercial ([arXiv 2609.29146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29146)). A purity guard should regenerate once, then fall back to a template, if `say` contains Latin or Devanagari characters beyond an allowlist of doctor names and loanwords. A lexical check should flag Kolkata forms (জল, "-টে" clock suffix) where Bangladeshi forms (পানি, চারটা) are expected.

On the speech side, very short replies like "হ্যাঁ" or "ok" are the hardest LID case. Apply turn-level hysteresis: keep the previous turn's language unless the new turn's {bn, en} probability is confident. NFC-normalise every transcript before it reaches the LLM or any scorer, and seed faster-whisper's `initial_prompt` or hotwords with doctor names, departments and common medicines. Phone audio needs special care. In a controlled Bangla telehealth study, 8 kHz audio raised WER from about **0.232 to 0.338**, because band-limiting cuts the energy of the sibilants /s/ and /ʃ/ ([MDPI Signals](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-6120/7/3/44)). The project's +6-point jump from clean to phone-plus-noise matches that. Low-confidence transcripts should trigger a confirmation turn ("আপনি কি বারো তারিখ বললেন?") rather than a booking. If a CTC model such as hishab's or Omnilingual's is adopted, note that CTC models output no punctuation, and that the hishab model scored **50.67% WER on long-form audio** despite its strong short-clip numbers ([arXiv 2605.08214](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08214v1)).

## Fine-tune the dialogue first, then the recogniser, then the voice

Every recipe below fits on 12 GB, but only with the serving stack stopped. On one card, training and live inference cannot run at the same time. Order the work by realism gained per GPU-hour. A QLoRA of the LLM on synthetic receptionist dialogues takes hours and most directly changes whether SHEFA "sounds like a receptionist". A Whisper LoRA changes whether it understands real callers. A custom voice is the slowest piece but the only route to a natural Bangladeshi voice that is licence-clean. No public Bangla appointment-booking dataset exists, so the dialogue data must be synthesised and then reviewed by Bangladeshi speakers.

The strongest argument for recording a voice talent is that data quality, not architecture, drives Bangla TTS naturalness. The same MB-iSTFT-VITS model went from **MOS 3.16 and 16.0% WER on 12 h of IndicTTS-Bn to MOS 4.46 and 9.5% WER on 20 h of professional single-speaker audio** ([arXiv 2609.29146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29146)). Every commercially clean public TTS corpus is Indian-accented, and none sounds like a receptionist. Pre-adapting on Rasa or IndicVoices-R and then fine-tuning on the talent's recordings combines phonetic coverage with the right accent. Avoid the licence traps in the production path: MMS-TTS (NC, inherited by fine-tunes) ([finetune-hf-vits](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ylacombe/finetune-hf-vits/main/README.md)), BanglaKontho (NC), IndicTTS (commercial use needs an MoU) ([CIS-India](https://voice.cis-india.org/indic-tts.html)), and the research-only Bangla IndicF5 fine-tune. Get a written voice-talent agreement covering commercial synthetic use. Train on real patient calls only with caller consent and de-identification.

## Free tiers cover a demo, but not stock-Whisper Bangla

A 30-minute demo needs roughly 60-90 LLM calls, about 10k TTS characters and about 900 s of caller audio. Every free tier below covers that volume. The real risks are shrinking quotas, preview-only features, and terms that allow training on free-tier data. The research also exposed a trap: **Groq's free Whisper is stock Whisper**. Stock large-v3 measured **70% WER** on Bangla in the project bench, and stock large-v3 scored 33.9% CER on FLEURS Bengali in an independent appendix ([Fleurs-SLU](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.06117)). large-v3-turbo shares large-v3's encoder, so it is probably no better. Groq Whisper is a good English fallback but cannot carry Bangla turns.

The best fallback is hybrid rather than all-cloud. The LLM is the largest VRAM item, while local OmiKhan STT is the best-measured Bangla recogniser. If the demo machine has any 4 GB-class GPU, keep ASR and Piper local and send only the LLM to **Gemini Flash free, with Groq llama-3.3-70b as the hot spare**. Both offer OpenAI-compatible endpoints. For a machine with no GPU, use **Azure F0 for bn-BD TTS and bn-IN STT** on one key, Groq Whisper for English turns, and Gemini or Groq for the LLM. Use only synthetic patients in demos, since Gemini's free terms allow human review and Bangladesh does not receive the EEA protections. Rehearse on a separate project and key so rehearsals do not use up the demo key's daily quota.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which new model is best" to "which measurement is missing". On every axis where SHEFA has its own numbers (tool routing, Bangla WER, TTS intelligibility), the incumbents hold up against 2026 releases. The public leaderboards that might overturn them test larger models, Indian Bengali, or clean audio, not Bangladeshi callers on 8 kHz lines. The two gaps that most threaten a natural-sounding demo are not about model choice: nobody has listened to the Bangla voice, and the English path barely exists. Both can be closed in a day with a listening panel and a 0.6 GB LID model.

The longer-term implication is that SHEFA's distinctiveness will come from data the project creates, not data it downloads. Every commercially clean public Bangla TTS corpus is Indian-accented, the only real telemedicine corpus has no stated licence, and no receptionist dialogue set exists. A few hours of a consenting Dhaka voice talent, a few thousand reviewed synthetic dialogues, and consented recordings of real clinic calls would give SHEFA something no 12 GB competitor can download. Those same recordings become the test set that settles every future model swap.
